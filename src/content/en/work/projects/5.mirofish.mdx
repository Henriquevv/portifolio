## 1. The Business Problem

The unpredictability of hospital demand is one of the biggest challenges in care management. In networks with multiple hospitals, complexity multiplies: each unit has its own profile, different seasonalities and patient flows that cross between sectors and between hospitals.

When it is not known in advance how many patients will arrive, which sectors will be full or how a seasonal event will impact the operation, the consequence is overcrowding in the ER, cancellation of surgeries and stress on teams.

This project was developed as a simulated case study of a hospital network in the Brazilian Northeast with two hospitals with different profiles: a large general hospital (Hospital A) with high occupancy and another originally specialized in cardiology (Hospital B), in transition to a general profile and with significant idle capacity.

## 2. Solution Proposal

Instead of using traditional statistical models (time series, regression) that treat the hospital as a "black box", I adopted an approach based on **Swarm Intelligence**.

The idea is simple: create a "digital world" where virtual agents — representing hospitals, sectors, teams and patients — interact freely with each other. The patterns that emerge from these interactions (bottlenecks, solutions, redistributions) are not programmed in advance.

**Why Swarm Intelligence for Hospitals?**

1. **Emergence**: Instead of programming each scenario, complex behaviors emerge naturally from interactions between simple agents.
2. **Adaptation**: The model adjusts to structural changes (such as a hospital's profile transition) without the need for reprogramming.
3. **Multi-perspective**: Each agent has their own "view" of the problem, generating solutions that consider multiple points of view simultaneously.

## 3. About MiroFish

![png](/images/projects/mirofish/mirofish3.png)

[MiroFish](https://github.com/666ghj/MiroFish) is an open-source swarm intelligence engine that works in 5 steps:

1. **Graph Construction**: Extracts entities and relationships from the seed document (seed material) and builds a knowledge graph (GraphRAG).
2. **Agent Generation**: Creates autonomous agents with personalities, memories and behaviors based on the graph.
3. **Simulation**: Agents interact on parallel platforms, posting, commenting and reacting.
4. **Report**: A ReportAgent analyzes interactions and generates structured predictions.
5. **Deep Interaction**: It is possible to "interview" agents to deepen insights.

MiroFish does not receive tabular data directly. It consumes **textual documents** (analytical reports) as seed material. This means that the raw data needs to be processed, analyzed and turned into a report before feeding the engine.

## 5. Data Validation

Each extraction revealed important particularities:

**Admissions and Discharges**: 11 columns. 71 patients not discharged (still hospitalized). 133 null CIDs (12.6% — normal for elective surgeries). Average stay of 5.5 days (median 1.5 — asymmetric distribution).

**Bed Occupancy**: Granularity at the bed level (1 line per bed/day), not by sector. Occupancy rate calculated by `SUM(QT_PAC_DIA) / SUM(QT_LEITO_DIA)`. 39% of records are blocked/inactive beds.

**Emergency Care**: Critical discovery — DT_HIGH from the PS is artificial (98% exactly 11h40min after entry). Field `IE_TIPO_ATENDIMENTO = 1` identifies PS→Admission conversions (14.8%).

**Emergency Times**: 18 time fields.

**Transfers**: Each line is a pass per sector, not pair origin→destination.

**Demographic Profile**: 100% crossing with Extractions 1 and 3.

## 8. Bed Occupancy

The most striking fact of the study: the brutal asymmetry between the two hospitals.

```python
# Ocupação agregada por setor/dia
df_ocu_agg = df_ocu.groupby(
    ['DT_REFERENCIA', 'DS_SETOR_ATENDIMENTO', 'CD_ESTABELECIMENTO']
).agg(
    LEITOS_ATIVOS=('QT_LEITO_DIA', 'sum'),
    PACIENTES=('QT_PAC_DIA', 'sum'),
).reset_index()

df_ocu_agg['TAXA_OCUPACAO'] = (
    df_ocu_agg['PACIENTES'] / df_ocu_agg['LEITOS_ATIVOS'].replace(0, np.nan) * 100
)
```

**Hospital A: 84.5% average occupancy**
- Block B wards (5th and 6th floors): often above **95%** — real bottlenecks
- ICU 2 (27 beds, largest ICU): average of **91%**
- Xth Floor 2: main simultaneous receiver of surgical and emergency flows

**Hospital B: 54.1% average occupancy**
- Significant idle capacity
- Adult hospitalization varies between 40% and 80%
- Growth trend over the 39 months (profile transition)

**PS Conversion→Hospitalization:**

- Hospital B: 43.7% — much higher rate, indicating that Hospital B's ED is predominantly used as a gateway for admissions

## 10. Times of the Patient Journey

The extraction of times revealed the patient's complete journey in the ER:

| Step | Average time |
|-------|---------------|
| Waiting for reception | 13 min |
| Registration | 7 min |
| Waiting for screening | 7 min |
| Screening (Manchester) | 1 min |
| **Door → Doctor** | **30 min** |
| Waiting for the doctor | 14 min |
| Medical care | 92 min |
| **Full EU stay** | **134 min (2.2h)** |

The door-to-doctor time of **30 minutes** is the key quality indicator of the ED. Patients who require backup (observation bed) stay on average for an additional 4 hours.

## 11. Flows and Transfers

82,263 transfers between different sectors, with 1,655 between the two hospitals.


**Two major flow patterns identified:**

1. **Surgical flow**: Surgical Center → PACU → Infirmary (Xth floor 2) — 10,040 + 5,076 transfers
2. **Emergency flow**: 1st Floor Adult (rear EU) → Higher wards or ICU — 2,321 for 7th floor

**Inter-hospital flow**: Concentrated on hemodynamics — patients undergo catheterization in Hospital A and are admitted to Hospital B, or vice versa.

## 12. Demographic Profile

Hospitals serve markedly different populations:

| Indicator | Hospital A | Hospital B |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Patients ≥65 years old | 16% | **41%** |
| Pediatrics (0-17) | 26% | 10% |
| Female | 57% | 61% |

Hospital B has a 2.5× older profile — with cardiological origins. This is fundamental for the simulation: agents representing patients from Hospital B must have behaviors compatible with an older population.

## 13. Specialized Hospital Transition

The evolution graph of specialties at Hospital B over the 39 months revealed:

- Maintains **75-85% cardiology** throughout the entire period
- Gradual growth of **surgical**: from ~10% (2023) to ~20-25% (2025-2026)
- Other specialties (medical clinic, pediatrics) appear intermittently (\<5%)
- Transition is **slow and partial** — good news for research

## 19. Limitations

**Limitations:**

- **Number of agents**: The simulation used only 2 agents. Simulations with 20-50 agents (representing individual sectors, specialties, shifts) would produce richer, more granular dynamics.
- **Qualitative in nature**: MiroFish generates qualitative projections (scenarios, strategies), not exact numerical predictions. The numbers cited in the results (20 min reduction, 94% threshold) are agent-emergent estimates, not statistical calculations.
- **Test data**: Test request/result timestamps not available — this limits modeling of the diagnostic bottleneck.
- **Free model**: A free LLM model was used via OpenRouter, which imposes rate limits and can affect the depth of agent interactions.
